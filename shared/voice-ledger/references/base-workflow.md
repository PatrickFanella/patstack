# voice-ledger

Use for an explicit voice guide, voice alignment review, or a marketing packet that lacks a usable voice reference. A small copy edit can use the existing guide without rebuilding it. Visual identity and logo production retain their brand/design owner.

Read the project's brand and voice sources first. Preserve an approved voice; propose changes when samples conflict instead of silently replacing it. Ask for missing cues when they materially change the direction, while drafting what the available sources support.

1. Identify the reader, task, and source samples. Distinguish user-provided preferences from your proposed direction.
2. Define a few memorable attributes as writing decisions: sentence shape, vocabulary, degree of humor, and technical detail. Include a concrete revision example for each. Do not invent customer language or the maker's personal experience.
3. Describe how the voice changes for promotion, onboarding, support, failure, and cancellation. Explain failures plainly; keep the next action accurate.
4. Record preferred product terms, retired names, and wording that would imply unsupported scope. A vocabulary list should explain the issue, not ban legitimate terms mechanically.
5. Apply the guide to a short product description, social post, and support message. Check that they sound related while serving different reader needs.

Save the guide in the project's existing brand/marketing location, link its source, date the review, and label proposed direction. Return the guide, examples, unresolved decisions, and source references. Writing a guide does not authorize a profile change or publication.
