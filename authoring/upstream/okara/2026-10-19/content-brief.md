# Content Brief

Write the brief, not the article. A great brief removes ambiguity so any
writer — human or AI — can execute it without a round of follow-up questions
and without missing the strategic intent.

## When to use
- You're assigning a piece to a writer or an AI and want it right the first time.
- You need consistency across writers and topics.
- You're planning a piece and want to lock intent, angle, and structure before drafting.
- Drafts keep coming back off-target and you want to fix it at the source.

## Inputs
- **Required:** the topic or target keyword and the business goal for the piece.
- **Optional:** audience research, competitor URLs, brand voice, and required keywords or links.

## Method
1. **Define the goal.** Target keyword, search intent, funnel stage, and the business objective the piece serves.
2. **Profile the audience.** Who they are, their pain and sophistication, and the one thing they must walk away with.
3. **Set the angle.** The specific take that differentiates this piece from what already ranks — the reason it deserves to exist.
4. **Specify the structure.** Working title options, the required H2s, and the key points or questions each section must answer.
5. **List the specs.** Word count, tone and voice, format, required entities and keywords, internal and external links, and the call to action.
6. **Attach references.** Sources to cite, competitors to beat, and data or examples to include.

## Output format
A one-page brief a writer can execute:
- **Goal** — keyword, intent, funnel stage, objective
- **Audience** — who, pain, key takeaway
- **Angle** — the differentiating take
- **Structure** — title options, required H2s, points per section
- **Specs** — word count, voice, keywords, links, CTA
- **References** — sources, competitors, data

## Quality rules
- A brief succeeds if a writer needs zero follow-up questions to start.
- Specify the angle, not just the topic — topic alone produces generic drafts.
- Give required H2s and points, not a full outline; leave room for the writer's craft.
- Every requirement should trace back to the goal; cut anything that doesn't.
