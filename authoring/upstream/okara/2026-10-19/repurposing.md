# Repurposing

One strong asset can become a dozen. This skill breaks a source piece into its
reusable parts and adapts each for a specific channel, so you get maximum reach
from work you've already done — without spamming the same paragraph everywhere.

## When to use
- You published something strong and want more mileage from it.
- You need a steady stream of social and email content without starting from scratch.
- You have a webinar, report, or podcast sitting as a single asset.
- You want each channel to get content shaped for that channel, not a link dump.

## Inputs
- **Required:** the source asset and the channels you want to repurpose it for.
- **Optional:** brand voice per channel, the priority channels, and a distribution timeframe.

## Method
1. **Pick the source.** Start from a high-performing or high-effort asset — a pillar post, webinar, report, or interview — that has ideas worth spreading.
2. **Extract the atoms.** Pull out the reusable units: stats, quotes, steps, frameworks, hot takes, and examples.
3. **Map ideas to formats.** Match each atom to the channel it fits — a thread, a carousel, a newsletter section, a short-video script, a quote graphic, a snippet.
4. **Adapt, don't paste.** Rewrite each unit for its channel's norms, length, and audience; a paragraph and a post are not the same shape.
5. **Add native hooks and CTAs.** Give each derivative a channel-appropriate opening and a next step, and link back to the source where it makes sense.
6. **Sequence distribution.** Schedule the derivatives across channels over time so one asset fuels weeks of output.

## Output format
A repurposing map:
- **Source asset** and its extracted atomic ideas
- Per derivative: **channel, format, adapted copy, hook, and CTA**
- A suggested **distribution sequence** across channels and time

## Quality rules
- Adapt to each channel; copy-pasting the same text everywhere underperforms on all of them.
- One idea per derivative — don't cram the whole source into a single post.
- Lead each piece with a channel-native hook, not the source's intro.
- Always give a next step; repurposed content should still drive somewhere.
