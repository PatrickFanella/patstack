# Brand Voice

"Friendly but professional" isn't a voice — it's a wish. A usable brand voice is
a small set of defined attributes with rules and examples that anyone, human or
agent, can write from and produce copy that sounds like one company.

## When to use
- Your voice is described in adjectives no one can act on.
- A growing team's writing sounds like several different companies.
- You're onboarding freelancers or agents who need a reference.
- You want to audit and align existing copy to one voice.

## Inputs
- **Required:** a few descriptors of how you want to sound, or samples of copy
  you like.
- **Optional:** your audience, existing guidelines, and examples of off-brand
  writing to contrast against.

## Method
1. **Extract attributes.** Distill the cues into three to five concrete voice
   attributes (e.g., Warm, Clear, Confident). More than five is unusable.
2. **Define each one.** Write a one-line definition of what the attribute means
   in your writing — and what it does not.
3. **Show do/don't pairs.** For each attribute, give a "we write this, not that"
   example so the rule is unmistakable.
4. **Set tone shifts.** Note how the voice flexes by context — marketing vs.
   product UI vs. support — while staying recognizably you.
5. **Build a word list.** List words and phrases to use and to avoid, including
   jargon and clichés to cut.
6. **Ground it in the audience.** Tie the voice to who you speak to and the
   language they actually use.
7. **Apply and check.** Use the guide to write or revise a sample, then verify
   the output matches every attribute.

## Output format
- **Attributes**: three to five, each defined in a line.
- **Do/don't examples**: a before/after per attribute.
- **Tone map**: how voice shifts across contexts.
- **Vocabulary**: use and avoid lists.
- **Audience note**: who you're writing for.

## Quality rules
- Keep attributes few and concrete — a guide no one remembers isn't used.
- Every rule needs an example; abstractions don't transfer.
- Show the "don't," not just the "do."
- A voice guide is only real if it changes the next draft.
